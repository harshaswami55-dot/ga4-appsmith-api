# Sumlink Analytics - 1-Year Analytics Plan

Hi Nuthan,

Sure. Below is the clear 1-year analytics plan with a weekly sprint approach so we can build a strong hold on analytics in a structured way.

## Objective

The goal for the next 12 months is to establish a reliable analytics system that supports product, marketing, retention, monetization, and business decision-making through accurate tracking, validated dashboards, cohort analysis, funnel monitoring, and actionable reporting.

## Overall 1-Year Roadmap

## Quarter 1: Foundation and Data Reliability

Focus:
Stabilize tracking, data quality, dashboard accuracy, and reporting consistency.

Expected outcomes:
- Clean GA4 event structure
- Reliable Appsmith and backend reporting
- KPI definitions aligned with business understanding
- Reduced dependency on manual checking

Weekly sprint direction:
- Sprint 1: Audit existing events, dashboards, filters, KPIs, and data sources
- Sprint 2: Fix tracking gaps, naming inconsistencies, and broken calculations
- Sprint 3: Validate all major KPIs against GA4 and BigQuery
- Sprint 4: Standardize dashboard logic, documentation, and reporting process
- Sprint 5: Create alerting/checklist for broken pipelines and anomalies
- Sprint 6: Stakeholder review and KPI signoff

Deliverables:
- Analytics audit report
- KPI dictionary
- Validated executive dashboard
- Data quality checklist
- Tracking issue backlog

## Quarter 2: Funnel and User Journey Analytics

Focus:
Understand acquisition quality, onboarding flow, and drop-off points.

Expected outcomes:
- Clear visibility on user journey from install to activation
- Funnel-level drop-off analysis
- Better identification of weak onboarding and conversion points

Weekly sprint direction:
- Sprint 1: Build acquisition source-level performance views
- Sprint 2: Build onboarding funnel and tutorial completion analysis
- Sprint 3: Analyze player drop-off by stage, level, and early session behavior
- Sprint 4: Segment by geography, app version, device, and channel
- Sprint 5: Add benchmark views and comparison periods
- Sprint 6: Product recommendations based on findings

Deliverables:
- Acquisition dashboard
- Onboarding funnel dashboard
- Early behavior analysis
- Segment comparison views
- Funnel insights summary

## Quarter 3: Retention, Cohorts, and Engagement

Focus:
Build stronger retention understanding using GA4 and BigQuery.

Expected outcomes:
- Day 1, Day 3, Day 7, Day 15+ retention visibility
- Rolling retention and cohort-level analysis
- Engagement behavior tied to retention trends

Weekly sprint direction:
- Sprint 1: Validate retention logic and cohort formulas
- Sprint 2: Build exact BigQuery-based cohort retention models
- Sprint 3: Add rolling retention and manager summary tables
- Sprint 4: Correlate retention with onboarding and gameplay behavior
- Sprint 5: Build repeat-user and churn-risk indicators
- Sprint 6: Present retention improvement recommendations

Deliverables:
- Retention dashboard
- BigQuery cohort validation
- Rolling retention model
- Engagement-retention correlation report
- Churn-risk indicators

## Quarter 4: Automation, Optimization, and Decision Support

Focus:
Turn analytics into a repeatable decision system for teams.

Expected outcomes:
- Automated weekly and monthly reporting
- Faster decision-making for product and growth teams
- Better experiment-readiness and outcome tracking

Weekly sprint direction:
- Sprint 1: Automate recurring reports for stakeholders
- Sprint 2: Build anomaly and movement summary reporting
- Sprint 3: Add experiment measurement framework
- Sprint 4: Create manager-level KPI summary and health views
- Sprint 5: Improve performance, governance, and ownership model
- Sprint 6: Final annual review and next-year planning

Deliverables:
- Automated reporting workflow
- Experiment analytics framework
- Executive health review pack
- KPI monitoring cadence
- Annual analytics summary

## Weekly Sprint Structure

Each sprint can follow this pattern:
- Day 1: Requirement alignment and sprint planning
- Day 2: Data extraction, event review, and logic design
- Day 3: Dashboard/query/backend implementation
- Day 4: Validation against GA4/BigQuery
- Day 5: Review, fixes, documentation, and stakeholder update

## Key Workstreams Across the Year

- Tracking and event governance
- Dashboard stability and usability
- KPI validation and business alignment
- Funnel and conversion analytics
- Cohort and retention modeling
- Gameplay and engagement insights
- Automated reporting
- Experiment and optimization analytics

## Success Metrics

By the end of the year, success should look like:
- 95%+ KPI consistency across dashboard and source systems
- Faster weekly reporting turnaround
- Clear visibility into acquisition, onboarding, gameplay, and retention
- Reduced analytics confusion across teams
- Stronger product decisions supported by validated data
- Defined ownership and analytics process for ongoing scale

## Risks and Dependencies

Dependencies:
- Stable event tracking in GA4
- BigQuery access and export continuity
- Business approval on KPI definitions
- Stakeholder availability for periodic validation

Risks:
- Event schema changes without documentation
- Inconsistent naming across product and analytics
- Dashboard logic diverging from source truth
- Delays in access, approvals, or instrumentation updates

## Conclusion

This 1-year plan is designed to move analytics from reactive reporting to a dependable decision-making system. The first phase will focus on trust and accuracy, the second on user journey visibility, the third on retention intelligence, and the final phase on automation and optimization. With weekly sprint execution, we can steadily improve analytics maturity and create stronger control over business and product insights.
